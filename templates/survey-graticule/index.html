01 — What we do

Eight things, done properly.

A survey practice that claims everything usually subcontracts most of it. This is the list we hold in-house, with our own instruments and our own people, and it is the list we will quote against.

01

Topographic survey

→

Detail and levels across open ground, from a half-hectare infill plot to a forty-hectare coastal realignment. Delivered as a survey-grade DWG with a separate levelled surface, not a flat drawing with spot heights typed on.

OS grid or site grid1:200 to 1:1000±15 mm typical 02

Measured building survey

→

Plans, sections and elevations of what is actually standing, including the parts that are out of plumb. Historic fabric is scanned before it is drawn, so the drawing can be checked against the cloud rather than against memory.

Level 1 to Level 4Revit or DWGListed-building experience 03

Terrestrial laser scanning

→

Registered point clouds for retrofit, clash detection and record. We publish the registration report with the cloud, so you can see the residual on every setup instead of taking the alignment on trust.

E57 & RCPRegistered < 4 mmPanoramic imagery 04

Bathymetric & hydrographic survey

→

Single-beam and multibeam work in the estuaries and harbours along this coast, tied to the same datum as the land survey so the two halves of a sea wall meet in the middle. Tidal reduction from our own gauge, not an assumed curve.

IHO Order 1aReduced to ODN & CDVessel to 8 m 05

UAV photogrammetry

→

Aerial capture where ground access is slow, unsafe or would take a week — quarry faces, roofs, forestry tracks, active earthworks. Always flown against ground control, because a flight without it is a picture, not a survey.

CAA operational authorisationOrthomosaic & DSM±25 mm to control 06

Setting out & as-built

→

Control networks, setting out to the design model, and the as-built check that proves what went in. We keep the network adjusted for the life of the job, so month eleven is measured from the same points as week one.

Least-squares adjustedSame-day as-builtsDeviation reporting 07

→

Movement watched over months on retaining structures, quay walls and buildings next to deep excavation, with agreed trigger levels and a named person who phones when one is crossed rather than emailing a spreadsheet.

02 — Accuracy

How close is close enough?

Nobody needs every job at three millimetres, and paying for it when you don’t is the most common waste in this industry. The honest answer depends on what the measurement is going to decide.

These are the working tolerances of the instruments we run, at the ranges we actually run them — not the headline figure off the brochure.

04 — How a job runs

Six stages, and you hear from us at each one.

The order matters. Most survey disputes are really control disputes, and control is established before anyone measures a single point of detail.

01

Brief

What the survey has to decide, who receives it, and in which software it has to open.

02

Recon

A walk-over before a price. Access, sightlines, tides, livestock, and what the site is hiding.

03

Control

A network observed and least-squares adjusted, with witness diagrams, before any detail.

04

Field

Detail, levels, scans and soundings, checked into the network as they are collected.

05

Adjust

Reduction, adjustment and a residual report. If something did not close, you are told.

06

Deliver

Drawing, model and report together, with the control schedule so the next firm can pick it up.

05 — The archive

Every job is a sheet, and we keep it.

Nineteen hundred jobs since 2009 are held as adjusted networks with their raw observations, not as flattened PDFs. When a client comes back seven years later because a boundary is being argued or an extension is going in, we re-issue from the original data on the original datum.

It also means a repeat visit is cheaper than a first one, sometimes by half: the control is already there, already witnessed, and still adjusts.
