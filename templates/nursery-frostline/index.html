Why it matters

A plant raised warm has to learn your winter. Ours already knows it.

Most perennials reach a garden centre from a heated Dutch glasshouse in February, in leaf, six weeks ahead of the season. They look better than ours on the bench. They are also the ones that go through a hard February and do not come back, and by then nobody connects the two.

        The growing ground

        Eleven acres, no roof.

        Stock stands in open ground and in unheated Dutch lights that are shut against rain, never against cold. We lift bare-root between November and March, which is when the plant is asleep and does not notice, and we do not lift at all in a hard frost because a frozen root ball breaks rather than bends.

        The ground here grows Dryas octopetala wild on the limestone above the top field. If it lives on that bank without us, it will live in your garden.
