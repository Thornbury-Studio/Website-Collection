Schools and groups

Learning

We take about 4,000 pupils a year. The aim is not to cover the curriculum — your classroom does that better. It is to put a real instrument in front of them and let them take a measurement nobody knows the answer to yet.

01Programmes

What a visit looks like

Every programme includes a dome show and a gallery session. The difference is what the practical half is, and how much of it is measurement rather than demonstration.

Half day is roughly three hours; full day is 10:00 to 14:30 including lunch.

02Why it works

Real data, not a worksheet

The Variable Stars day uses frames from our own archive that have not been reduced yet. When a class measures a light curve, that measurement goes into the programme with the school’s name against it.

Roughly one class in twelve finds something the automated pipeline missed — usually a bad frame, occasionally something real. In 2024 a Year 12 group from Workington caught a flare on an M dwarf that our own reduction had clipped as noise.

Fourteen-year-olds can tell the difference between doing science and being shown science, and they are much more interested in the first one.
