# COIL — product truth

This file plus this template's `DESIGN.md` (which inherits
`../../../DESIGN-SYSTEM/DESIGN.md`) is what any design or copy pass reads
first. Every number below is also in `js/site.js` (`CATALOGUE` and `STEEPS`);
the page renders prices, weights, times and the per-cup sum from there, never
from typed copy, and `node tools/check-figs.mjs` fails if an HTML fallback
drifts from them.

## Who this is for
Someone who drinks tea from bags and suspects they are missing something, or
who already brews loose leaf and wants one good oolong rather than a menu.
They can't taste the difference between two descriptions. They *can* see
whether a leaf is whole. Second reader: a gift buyer who wants one tin.

## The one claim
A whole leaf, rolled tight, opens back toward its shape in hot water, a little
further every time it is steeped, so it gives five good cups. Leaf that was
cut to dust has no shape left to open, gives everything at once and is done
after one. The page shows this with one real photograph of the same tea dry
and opened, and a teabag's contents at the same size beside the opened leaf.

## Real-world facts the copy relies on
- Taiwanese ball-rolled oolong is wrapped in cloth, pressed and rolled,
  loosened and rolled again, repeatedly (sources give up to about twenty
  rounds), over one to two days. Rolling is what lets a whole leaf travel
  without breaking.
- Before rolling: sun-withering, tossing to bruise the leaf edges (which
  starts oxidation), and heating to stop it ("kill green").
- A first steep of a tightly rolled oolong takes roughly 3 to 5 minutes, as
  the pellet opens. The second can be shorter because the leaf is already
  open; after that, each steep gets longer.
- Jin Xuan has a naturally milky, buttery note. Nothing is added for it.
- In Taiwan, "high-mountain tea" conventionally means gardens above about
  1,000 m. Lugu Township is known for oolong; Xitou, within Lugu, sits at
  about 1,150 m.

## Voice & tone
Sensory and specific, short sentences, calm. Let the physical fact do the
persuading. Banned: "artisanal", "ceremony", "zen", "mindful", "journey"
(as a section name or otherwise), and any adjective a buyer couldn't check in
their own cup. Taste notes are ordinary food words (cream, orchid, butter,
sugarcane, warm rice, pear, stone, salt), one line per steep.

## Not claimed
No farmer, family or producer is named or implied. No certification, award,
review, founder story or team photograph. None of the photographs is
presented as taken at COIL's garden except the Lugu fog, which was taken in
Lugu. The tin photograph is a real, unlabelled tin with COIL's label
composited on; it is presented as the product, which is what a label
printer's mock-up is.
