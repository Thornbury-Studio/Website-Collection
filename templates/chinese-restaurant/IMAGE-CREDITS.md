# Bái Hè 白鶴 — credits & sourcing

## Status of this template

**Bái Hè (白鶴, "white crane") is a fictional restaurant.** The name, the menu, the prices, the
address and the copy were all written for this template. Nothing here can be ordered and no real
business is being represented — unlike the `entertainment-triggered` template, which is an explicit
redesign of a real venue and is handled under much stricter rules.

The dishes themselves are real, standard Cantonese and Sichuan dishes with their conventional Chinese
names and pinyin. That is culinary vocabulary, not anyone's intellectual property.

## Imagery

**There is no photography in this template at all.** Every dish is an original top-down vector
illustration generated by [`tools/gen-plates.js`](tools/gen-plates.js) — a build-time script; nothing
loads it at runtime. Re-running it (`node tools/gen-plates.js plates.json`) regenerates the SVG for
re-inlining.

That was a design decision before it was a licensing one. The same artwork has to work in three
places at once:

1. at 62px on a menu row,
2. at roughly 26% of the table when a dish is placed,
3. and on a white page, where the muddy cut-out edges of a food photograph would show badly.

Flat top-down vector does all three, and it means the plate you tap is recognisably the plate that
lands on the table — which is the whole conceit of the page. A photo set could not hold that
relationship without a second set of assets.

Each dish is emitted once as an SVG `<symbol>` and referenced with `<use>` from the menu row and from
every copy on the table. Note the constraint that comes with `<use>`: **a `<use>` instance does not
pick up CSS rules matching the referenced original** (this bit the `entertainment-triggered` template
badly). It is safe here only because the plate artwork is entirely static — every animation lives on
the wrapper element, never inside the symbol.

## The scatter helper

`scatter()` in the generator uses a small deterministic PRNG rather than `Math.random()`, so rice
grains, sesame and chilli flakes land in the same place on every build. Otherwise every regeneration
would produce a spurious diff across all twelve dishes.

## The table

The round table is the point of the template: a menu is a control surface and the table is its
readout. Dishes ring the lazy susan, the ring and the plates both shrink as the table fills so ten
dishes still fit, place settings redraw with the party size, and the advice line reads the
composition back the way a waiter would — one dish per person, always a green, rice with the mains.

Hovering a menu row drops a dashed ghost plate into the next slot and shuffles the existing plates to
make room, so you can see the arrangement before committing to it. That preview is bound to fine
pointers only: on touch there is no hover, and firing it on tap would flash a ghost for one frame
before the real plate appeared.

## Typography

Noto Serif SC (Google Fonts) carries both the Latin display type and the Chinese, so the two never
disagree about weight or colour on the page — a common failure when a Latin serif is paired with a
fallback CJK face. Inter handles UI text.
