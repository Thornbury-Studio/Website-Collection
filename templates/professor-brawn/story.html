Our story

First, it was one stall.

Two job coaches, a folding table and a handful of trainees who were ready to work, not ready to be trained forever. Common Ground started as a weekend stall, because a classroom can only prove so much — a paying customer proves the rest.

Every Hand Trained

From one café to a movement

Thirteen years of showing what's possible

  1. 2013

    Two job coaches open a weekend stall

    Frustrated that skilled trainees had nowhere real to prove themselves, two job coaches set up a folding table on weekends — coffee, toast, whatever the trainees could confidently cook. It sold out most Saturdays within the hour.

  2. 2016

    The stall becomes a proper café

    Enough regulars had asked for a real seat that the stall moved indoors — a small shopfront in Ang Mo Kio, open five days a week, with a rostered team instead of a rotating one. It was the first time anyone on the team had a payslip with their name on it.

  3. 2019

    Bridgework Singapore comes on board

    Running one café well is one thing; training a steady pipeline of new hires is another. Bridgework Singapore, an employment-support programme, partnered with us that year to formalise the training — job coaches, a proper onboarding track, and a plan for a second kitchen.

  4. Today

    Two kitchens — Ang Mo Kio and Tampines

    Common Ground now runs public cafés in Ang Mo Kio and Tampines. Team members train and work with support from a Bridgework Singapore job coach, alongside colleagues and customers who treat the counter like any other café counter — because that’s exactly what it is.

Inclusion in action

A real shift, not a photo op

Every service is staffed by an inclusive team of different abilities, ages and socio-economic backgrounds — real training, real wages, real careers. Your order is what funds it.

Beyond the kitchen

A shift that counts as real experience

Every trainee who completes a season at Common Ground leaves with a reference, a logged set of hours, and — for most — a job offer somewhere else in food service if they want one. A few have stayed and now train the next intake themselves.

We’re audited each year against Singapore’s standard for disability-inclusive employers, the same bar any serious inclusive employer here works toward.
