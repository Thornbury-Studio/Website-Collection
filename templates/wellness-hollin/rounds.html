The rounds

Four rooms

90 minutes, 3 rounds, the same four rooms each time, always in the same order. Here is what each one is for, and how long you stay.

Hot room

80–90 °C

Three tiers of aspen benches. The higher you sit, the hotter it is: 80 °C at your feet on the bottom tier, 90 °C at the top.

Every ten minutes one of us puts water on the stones. The steam comes off them as a wave, rolls along the ceiling and comes down on the top bench first. If that's too much, move down a tier; nobody minds.

12 minutes is a round. If you need to leave sooner, leave sooner.

Rinse

38 °C

Four open showers between the heat and the water, held at 38 degrees.

A minute is plenty. The rinse keeps the plunge clean for the next person and takes the edge off the step from 90 to 4. It is the only rule we are strict about.

Water
38 °C
Time
1 minute
Rule
Every time, before every plunge

Cold plunge

4 °C

2 steel tubs, 1.2 metres deep, chilled to 4 °C and filtered between every session.

Step in, sit down, shoulders under. The first ten seconds are the hard part; your breath will try to run away from you. Let it out slowly. First round, stay 1 minute. After that, up to 2.

There's always someone from Hollin on the floor by the tubs. Nobody plunges alone.

Rest room

21 °C

A long low room at 21 degrees, 14 loungers, a blanket on each, and water on the side.

This is where the round actually happens: lying still while your skin warms back up from the inside. Give it 10 minutes. There is no music and no screen, and the lights stay low.

A whole session

90 minutes

Times are a guide, not a rule. You can sit out a round in the rest room, or stop after two.

Before you book. Contrast bathing isn't for everyone. If you are pregnant, or have a heart condition or high blood pressure, ask your doctor first. Don't come after drinking. We make no medical claims for any of this.
