Hollowgate Junction · Thrushmoor · West Riding

Frame No. 21 · C&TR pattern of 1897

Thirty-two spaces, sixteen levers, one argument.

The frame was supplied with thirty-two levers for a junction that was busier than it ever became. Sixteen are connected today; the rest stand white, and the numbering has never been closed up, because renumbering a frame means re-cutting the locking.

§ 01 — THE ROLL OF LEVERS

What each lever works.

Every lever carries an enamelled plate at eye level with its number, its colour and its job. This is that set of plates, read left to right along the frame.

§ 02 — THE LOCKING

A notch, a bar, and a piece of steel that says no.

Nothing in the tray under the frame is electrical, and nothing in it is clever. It is a set of flat bars that can only slide when the notches line up.

The consequence is the whole of railway safety as it was understood in 1897, and it is still the reason a mechanical box is trusted: the signalman is not asked to remember which combinations are dangerous. The combinations that are dangerous simply cannot be made. A wrong route is not an error that gets caught. It is a movement that does not happen.

It also explains the one thing visitors find strangest. You cannot put a lever back whenever you like. If lever 2 is off, then 7 is trapped reverse, because 2 was only allowed off on the strength of 7 being off first. The frame unwinds in the reverse of the order it was wound up, and it will wait all day.

§ 03 — THE LOCKING TABLE

The same relationship, written down twice.

Read a row across. Holds is what this lever pins where it stands the moment it is pulled — and, read the other way, what has to be standing that way before it will move at all. Both is a bolt, which holds in either direction and is not held back. Held by is whoever is sitting on this lever now.

A frame is only as good as this document, which is why we draw one for every box we overhaul and then prove it lever by lever before we sign it off. An undocumented frame is not a safe frame, however well it is greased.
