# KIAM 咸 — product truth

The single source for every fact a page states. `js/site.js` carries the
catalogue in code (names, colours, sugar, prices) and every figure on a
page is rendered from it; this file is the human-readable copy of the
same numbers. Change one, change both.

## Who this is for
People in Singapore who already pay S$4–6 for a cold-pressed juice, a
kombucha or a Fever-Tree, and who would rather drink less sugar than a
sweetener. They drink it cold from the bottle, over ice with a meal, or
as a mixer. They know what a sour plum in a lime juice does, even if
they have never thought about why.

## What it is
Five salted sodas in 250 ml glass bottles with a crown cap. Fresh
fruit juice or infusion, carbonated water, cane sugar, half a pinch
(150 mg) of sea salt per bottle. About a third of the sugar of an
ordinary soda: 7–9 g per bottle against roughly 26 g in a can of cola.
Made and bottled by two people, Shu Yi and Arif, in a small unit in
Tai Seng, in runs of 300 bottles. Pasteurised, so it keeps three months
in the fridge; unopened it is fine at room temperature but tastes best
very cold.

## Story, in order (it is a real sequence, so the story page may date it)
- 2022, December — Shu Yi starts salting the lime juice at a friend's
  café in Joo Chiat because the customers keep asking for "less sweet".
- 2023, January — first Saturday stall; three flavours; 120 bottles sold
  in four hours.
- 2023, August — the Tai Seng unit; a second-hand 2-head counter-pressure
  filler; runs of 300.
- 2024, March — Roselle joins; Arif comes on full time.
- 2025, June — Watermelon as the first seasonal; eight stockists.

## Voice & tone
Plain, exact, a little dry. Numbers over adjectives. Sentence case.
Never "crafted", "artisanal", "journey", "elevate", "curated", "handpicked".
The reviewers on the reviews page are quoted the way they wrote —
short, specific, occasionally ungrammatical — and attributed by first
name and where they drank it.
