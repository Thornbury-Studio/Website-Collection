The Centenary, examined

I

The dial

Champagne, cut into sectors the way we drew them in 1926. The centre is engine-turned on a hand-cranked rose lathe — one hundred and forty concentric passes, no two dials identical. The indices are solid brass, mirror-lapped, set by hand.

Guilloché · applied indices · heat-blued steel hands

II

The calibre

Calibre AV‑26, wound by crown, finished whether you can see it or not. Gilt bridges striped by hand, screws blued over an open flame, forty hours of reserve. The exhibition back was a controversial choice in-house. Then everyone saw it.

Manual wind · 40h reserve · 19 jewels

III

The wear

A tank case sits flat under a cuff — that was the whole argument for it in 1926, and it hasn’t aged a day. At 7.4 mm it disappears until candlelight finds it. Then it does what brass under candlelight does.

7.4 mm profile · 18k gilt brass · espresso alligator

Collectors’ words

  1. 1926

    A four-man atelier drafts a rectangular calibre because round cases “waste the sleeve.” Two hundred pieces are made. Then the ledgers go quiet.

  2. 1969

    The workshop closes with the quartz tide rising, its tooling sealed in the family vault — drawings, lathes, and one unfinished dial.

  3. 2026

    The vault is opened for the centenary. Same drawings, same lathe, same argument — one hundred years on, wound by hand.
