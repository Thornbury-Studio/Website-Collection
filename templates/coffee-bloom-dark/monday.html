The roast

Light, and the same every week.

One coffee means one roast profile, and fifty-two chances a year to get it exactly the same. We roast it light, for filter, and pull it at 204 °C, about ninety seconds after first crack.

Six batches, one log

Every batch is logged against the one before it: charge temperature, time to first crack, time from crack to drop. If batch five runs eight seconds long, it goes in the log and someone finds out why before next Monday.

We do not blend batches to average them out. Each bag carries its batch number beside the date.

Sealed before lunch

Beans rest in open bins for two hours to let the first rush of gas out, then go into valve bags: the gas can leave, air cannot get in.

The date goes on in red by hand, because a printed date is the easiest thing in the world to leave on a bag for a year.

The measurement

Every Monday we brew the last five roasts.

One dose from the new roast, and one from each of the four before it, bloomed side by side with 15 g of grounds and 45 g of water at 94 °C. We read the rise off a steel rule at the centre of the bed and time how long the dome holds above half its height. Three lots of those readings give the curve below; the simulation on the home page runs on it.

Whole bean only

We will not grind it for you.

Grinding opens up the inside of the bean. The gas that would have taken a month to leave a whole bean is mostly gone from ground coffee within the hour, and it takes the aromatics with it.

A bag we ground on Monday would bloom on Thursday like the one on the right of the home page. A cheap burr grinder does more for your coffee than anything we could do in the roastery.

The green room

The rest of the lot waits, green, at 17 °C.

Cool, dark, and dry

Green coffee is a seed, and it keeps like one. In lined sacks, in a room held at 17 °C and 55 per cent humidity, it will taste almost the same in March as it did when it landed.

Each Monday we weigh out 72 kilos, and that is the only coffee in the building that is ever older than a week.

Keeping it at home

  • Keep it in the bag it came in, valve up, rolled down tight.
  • Out of the sun and away from the kettle. Not in the fridge: it sweats every time you take it out.
  • If it is going to last you longer than a month, freeze half of it on the day it arrives, in a jar, and do not refreeze it.
