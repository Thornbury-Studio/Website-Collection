Model 77

LACQUER 77

A direct-drive turntable named for the lacquer master — the last object that is still physically cut before a record becomes a record.

  • Brushed aluminium platter, 2.8 kg
  • Walnut veneer plinth, constrained-damp core
  • Nine-inch gimbal tonearm, adjustable VTA
  • ±0.02% WRMS speed stability

Scroll — or pick a component

01 — Platter

Mass before speed

The platter is machined from a single aluminium blank, brushed on the lathe so the grain runs with the rotation, not across it. Mass is the first anti-vibration device — everything else is tuning.

2.8 kg rotating at 33⅓ — inertia is the filter.

Alloy
6061-T6
Finish
Longitudinal brush
Mat
Wool felt, 3 mm

02 — Plinth

What the motor sits on

A constrained-layer core wrapped in quarter-sawn walnut veneer — stiff enough to refuse motor chatter, soft enough not to ring. The plinth is not furniture. It is the reference plane the stylus assumes.

Damping first. Decoration never.

Veneer
European walnut
Core
MDF + butyl constrained layer
Feet
Sorbothane, 12 dB at 80 Hz

03 — Tonearm

Nine inches, gimbal bearing

A medium-mass gimbal arm with adjustable VTA and azimuth. The cartridge is not included because your cut engineer already has an opinion — and that opinion is the point of a mastering deck.

Tracking force is a number, not a feeling.

230 mm
Bearing
Gimbal, preloaded

04 — Vinyl

Grooves that catch light

The lacquer is cut with a heated stylus into a blank that still smells of nitrocellulose. What you hear on vinyl is not a file — it is a geometry problem solved in microns.

74 µm pitch at the outer edge. Light knows.

33⅓ & 45 RPM

05 — Cover

Dust is a frequency

Four millimetres of cast acrylic, hinged at the rear, grounded so static does not ride the groove. Transmission is real — not a tinted plane pretending to be glass.

What you see through it is what is playing.
