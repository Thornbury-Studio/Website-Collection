04 — Studio

Studio

A studio in Singapore that builds the whole site, not a deck about it.

I — The charter

  1. 01

    Nothing here is inherited.

    Every standard in this studio had to be written, argued over and agreed on. None of it arrived with the furniture. That is slower than adopting a house style, and it is the reason we can tell you why any decision was made rather than that it is simply how things are done.

    PracticeThe internal charter runs to fourteen articles. This page is the part that concerns you.

  2. 02

    We would rather be understood than admired.

    A website can be impressive for five seconds. After that it has to be read, used on a phone with one thumb, and understood by someone who is not thinking about design at all. When those two ambitions disagree, the second one wins — every time, and in writing.

    PracticePriority when something has to give: usability, then information, then visual effect. Effects are cut first, never the other two.

  3. 03

    The second look is the one that counts.

    Anything survives a glance. We build for the moment after it — when the site is opened on an old phone, when another developer reads the code, when nobody has touched it for two years and it still has to work. That moment is unglamorous and it is the one we design for.

    PracticeEvery effect on this site can be switched off. Turn JavaScript off and the pages still work.

    Not a setting to find later. This throws the switch now: the field stops, the film unloads, every transition and scroll animation on the site goes with them, and the page you are reading carries on working.

  4. 04

    We say no in writing.

    We publish a service only once we are confident in it — two of ours are still unlisted for exactly that reason, marked so everywhere they appear. That costs us enquiries. Pretending otherwise would cost more.

    PracticeOptimization and SEO are marked in development on every page they appear on, including the one selling them.

  5. 05

    Failure is budgeted, not hidden.

    We expect some things not to work; that is written into how this studio operates, not buried halfway through. What we owe a client is not the pretence of never being wrong — it is that being wrong stays our problem, never theirs.

    PracticeOn delivery you receive the repository, the hosting, the domain and the mailbox. If you walk away, nothing stays with us.

II — What we are not the studio for

  • A logo on its own. We draw one when it is going on a site we are building.
  • A rebuild that has to be live in ten days. We will tell you what is actually possible in the time before you pay for any of it.
  • A site with nobody in the room who can decide. We will ask, and the answer has to come from someone.

IV — What we build on

  • Security

    Every page we ship names the handful of places it is allowed to load anything from, and refuses the rest. It is written into the page itself, not bolted on afterwards.

    CheckBelow is not a copy of the policy — it is the policy, split into its directives out of this page’s own head as it loaded. View source and compare it line for line.

  • Performance

    Weight is a design decision. The heavy part of a page arrives after the page is already usable, and a phone is never sent the file that was made for a desktop.

    CheckOpen the network panel and load the home page. It paints on a 20 kB still; the film behind it is fetched afterwards, from a smaller cut made for that screen.

  • Ownership

    Owning a site should mean being able to read it. What we hand over is written by hand and stays that way: no bundler output to reverse, no framework to learn before you can change a heading, nothing generated that you would have to regenerate.

V — What sits behind the site

        
Five services do the work. Here is what each one is actually for.

        

          

            
Vercel

            
Where the site lives. It is served from whichever machine is nearest the person
              opening it, rather than from one box in one country.

          

          

            
Supabase

            
Where the site remembers things. Bookings, orders, accounts, a menu you edit
              yourself — anything that has to survive between visits sits here, on an account
              that is yours.

          

          

            
Resend

            
The mail the site sends. The order confirmation, the copy of an enquiry, the
              reset link — sent from your own domain rather than from a form service nobody
              recognises.

          

          

            
OWASP ZAP

            
Checks the finished site for known issues before it goes live. It is a public,
              open-source scanner: it looks for the well-understood ways a site gets broken into,
              and we fix what it reports.

          

          

            
SonarCloud

            
Reads the code rather than the running site, and flags the patterns that are
              known to cause trouble later, so they surface before launch instead of after it.

          

        

        

          
Neither of the last two makes a site unbreakable. Nothing does,
            and nobody can honestly promise it.

          
What they mean is that the known problems have been looked for,
            with public tools you are free to point at the site yourself.
