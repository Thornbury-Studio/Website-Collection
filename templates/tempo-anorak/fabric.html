Range Fabric Field notes

Garrow Mill · Ardsleigh · West Yorkshire

Fabric

Four cloths. Two mills. One very long wait.

A six-piece range is not modesty, it is arithmetic. We buy cloth in quantities small enough that every metre has to earn its place, which means we can only responsibly know four of them — where they are spun, how they behave after forty washes, and what they do when you are cold and forty minutes from home.

GR‑118

Grid fleece

Knitted as a waffle rather than a flat pile, so the fibre sits in raised islands with open channels between them. The islands trap warm air against the skin; the channels give sweat somewhere to go that is not your sleeve. It is an old idea done properly, which is most of what we buy.

We tested nine grid fleeces by washing each one forty times and measuring the recovery between cycles. Seven were slack by the twenty-fifth. This one was not.

AX‑96

2.5-layer laminate

Rated to a 20,000 mm water column, which is more rain than Yorkshire has ever managed on its best day. That is not why we bought it. We bought it for the 20,000 g of water vapour it moves through a square metre in a day, because the shell you take off halfway round is the shell that failed, however dry it kept you for the first mile.

The same 40-denier yarn, woven double and left unlaminated, is the cloth in Dry Spell and Low Sun. Buying one yarn twice is how a four-cloth list covers six garments.

MN‑92

Open mesh knit

Mostly hole. The interest is in the structure holding the holes apart: fine enough that a race number pinned through it in four places does not start a ladder, open enough that you can read a wristwatch through the back panel. Every mesh we rejected failed one of those two tests, usually the first.

This is the cloth we waited fourteen months for, and it is the reason Dew Point took four samples rather than two.

PW‑240

Four-way power knit

Dense enough to stay opaque when you crouch to tie a lace, which is a lower bar than most of the industry clears and the first thing we test. Recovery is measured on a rig in the unit: a hundred hours of cyclic load at knee extension, then a remeasure. This one comes back within two per cent of its cut length. The one we used in the first sample came back at nine.
