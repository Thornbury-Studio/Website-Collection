The range at length

Eight cloths, and the draft for each.

Every cloth below is drawn from its own draft at the sett we weave it, and the draft is written out beside it the way we write it on the loom. Prices are by the metre at 150 cm finished width, minimum two metres. Open any draft in the drafting room and change it; we will weave what you change.

01 / 08 · 2/2 twill

Kirkbrae Twill

The mill’s working cloth, and the one the second loom is nearly always threaded for. Walnut warp, oat weft: the diagonal reads at arm’s length and goes at three metres, which is the right distance for a jacket to be plain. Straight draw on four shafts, treadled one, two, three, four. If you learn one draft, learn this one; half the range is this draft with a different colour order.

02 / 08 · Broken 2/2 twill, 16-end repeat

Ettrick Herringbone

Eight ends threaded up the shafts, eight threaded down, and a break where they meet so the chevrons do not come to a point. The break is the whole difference between a herringbone and a goose eye, and it is one end. Peat on ecru; the back of the cloth is the same cloth the other way round, which makes it the one we recommend for anything unlined.

03 / 08 · 2/2 twill, four and four colour-and-weave

Hound

The tooth is not printed and not embroidered. It is what a 2/2 twill does when you give it four dark ends, four light ends, and the same again in the weft. Change either count and it stops being a hound; change the tie-up to tabby and it becomes a plain check. The structure is Kirkbrae. Only the yarn order is different, and the yarn order is everything.

04 / 08 · 2/2 twill, six and six colour-and-weave

Shepherd Check

The same structure as Hound with two more threads in every block, which is enough to turn teeth into a check. Woad and ecru, because that is what the hills wore before anyone thought to sell it. At six and six the twill line shows inside each block as a faint diagonal; at eight and eight it would show more, and the check would start to look like a plaid.

05 / 08 · 2/2 twill, four-and-four with two-and-two, madder overcheck

Glen

Two blocks of four-and-four, four blocks of two-and-two, then a pair of madder ends to close the repeat. Thirty-four ends across and thirty-four picks down, and because the block boundaries cross each other at every combination, it never looks the same twice. Set two ends closer than the rest of the range and clear cut rather than raised, so the small checks stay legible.

06 / 08 · Plain weave, colour-and-weave

Log Cabin

Plain weave, dark and light alternating, with the order swapped every eight ends and every eight picks. Nothing in the structure changes: every pick is over one, under one. The blocks are a colour trick — where the swaps agree you see vertical lines, where they disagree you see horizontal ones — and it is the oldest trick there is. The lightest cloth we make, and the only one we scour without milling.

07 / 08 · Rosepath, treadled as drawn in

Rosepath

An eight-end threading that turns back on itself: one, two, three, four, one, four, three, two. Treadled in the order it is threaded — the mill says as drawn in — it gives the small rose that Scandinavian looms have been giving for three hundred years. Treadle it as a straight twill instead and you get a broken diagonal; we sell that too, if you ask.

08 / 08 · 8-shaft point twill

Yarrow Diamond

Fourteen ends to the point on eight shafts, treadled to the same point. Every pick lifts four and drops four, so the cloth is a 2/2 twill folded into itself, and the diamonds are where the folds meet. This is the one cloth in the range the four-shaft loom cannot weave, which is why the eight-shaft loom exists.
