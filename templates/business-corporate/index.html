Three practices, and the problem each one is for

Most engagements begin in one and end up borrowing from another. They are staffed off the same bench, so that stays a conversation rather than a change order.

01

Strategy & Growth

For a company whose growth has quietly become harder to explain.

Where to compete, what to stop doing, and what the next three years have to prove. We argue the case from evidence you can check, not from a market you would like to be in.

  • Position, tested against the customer you actually have
  • Range and portfolio architecture
  • A three-year plan with named owners

02

Financial Advisory

For a board that no longer trusts the number it is being shown.

Budgeting, forecasting and capital planning rebuilt so the figures hold up under questioning — including a working model your own finance team can run without us in the room.

  • Rebuilt forecast, with the assumptions named
  • Capital and investment sequencing
  • A model your team owns and maintains
See the practice ↗
03

Operations Excellence

For a business that has outgrown the way it was run at half the size.

Process redesign that starts on the floor, in the depot or on the line — because the gap is almost never between the plan and the market, it is between the plan and Tuesday morning.

  • Process mapped where the work happens
  • Cost and cycle-time reduction, measured
  • An operating rhythm managers can run
