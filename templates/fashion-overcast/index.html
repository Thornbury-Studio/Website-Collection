The Grey Hours

Mid-weight layering for the three hundred days a year that aren’t photogenic.

Shop the collection

We make clothes for weather that can’t make up its mind, in a palette borrowed from the sky that produced it.

OVERCAST began with a complaint: everything sold to us for autumn was either too thin to matter or too heavy to wear indoors. So we made the layer in between — eight pieces, cut generously, in cloth chosen to be warm at nine degrees and bearable at seventeen.

Nothing here is seasonal in the disposable sense. The collection is added to; it is not replaced.

Art direction

We photograph the cloth, not the pose

Most clothing is sold on a body you will never have, in a light you will never stand in. We found that unhelpful. So every garment here is shot the way you actually meet it — close enough to read the weave, the stitch density, the way the yarn takes light.

One camera, one grade, one grey afternoon. If a piece can’t hold your attention at this distance, we don’t make it.

The way we work

Small runs, reordered

We cut between two and four hundred of each piece. When a size sells through we note it, and it returns in the next run rather than the next season — which is why a size is sometimes marked out rather than quietly deleted.

It makes forecasting harder and waste rarer. Everything is made within four hundred miles of the mill that supplied it.
