Origins

Six coffees. Six places on the line.

Every coffee we buy is dropped at its own point on the roast curve, chosen at the cupping table and held for the season. Below: the farm, the process, the altitude, and exactly where we stop the roast. Change your level and we tell you which of them are roasted there.

Ethiopia · WashedGuji, above HambelaRoasted at your level

Picked by hand on farms of a hectare or two, fermented for thirty-six hours, dried on raised beds. The kind of lot that tastes like fruit before it tastes like coffee. We take it light and stop forty seconds after first crack, so the bergamot survives.

Brewing

Three recipes we actually use.

Ratios are by weight. Water just off the boil for light roasts, a little cooler for dark. Grind is the variable you own; if it tastes sour go finer, if it tastes bitter go coarser.

Pour-over

Ratio
1 : 16
Dose
15 g / 240 g
Water
94 °C
Time
2:45 – 3:15

Bloom with twice the coffee’s weight for forty seconds, then two even pours. Best for the Guji and the Nyeri.

Espresso

Ratio
1 : 2
Dose
18 g / 36 g
Water
93 °C
Time
26 – 30 s

Rest the bag ten days first. The Cerrado and the Kerinci were dropped for this; the Huila gets there with a slightly longer shot.

French press

Ratio
1 : 14
Dose
30 g / 420 g
Water
92 °C
Time
4:00, then break the crust

Coarse grind, no plunge until you pour. The Dawn was built for this and for the moka pot.
